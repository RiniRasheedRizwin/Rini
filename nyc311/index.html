        Written from my review of October 2026. The figures quoted here are from that month; the other tabs show the latest data.

        1Fix the housing repair journey first

        EvidenceHousing (HPD) caused 56.6% of all late requests. Plumbing, door/window and water-leak complaints have a 3-day target, but only 25.7%, 14.5% and 14.1% closed on time.

        FixMap the value stream from complaint to inspection to repair and find where cases wait. Triage at intake: urgent repairs on a fast track, routine ones scheduled with a date the resident can see.

        2Re-baseline targets that measure the wrong thing

        EvidenceHomeless-assistance requests have a 1-hour target and only 8.2% met it.

        FixA target that is almost never met usually measures closure when it should measure first response. Define "responded" and "resolved" separately and set a target for each.

        3Work the ageing backlog on a fixed cadence

        Evidence4,546 Housing requests from October were still open more than 30 days later.

        FixA weekly ageing review, with automatic escalation at twice the target, stops cases from silently going stale.

        4Close the digital gap where calls are still common

        EvidenceConsumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) received only 58.4% of its requests online or by app, and Parks (DPR) 61.7%.

        FixGuided online forms with photo and location capture, plus status updates pushed to the resident, cut calls and repeat contacts: the same "effortless service" logic behind Abu Dhabi's TAMM.

        5Fix the data before the dashboard

        Evidence5,333 requests were closed in under a minute or before they were opened, and 6.9% had no recorded channel.

        FixStandard closure codes and mandatory channel capture make performance reporting trustworthy.

        About this project

        Rini Rasheed · Business Process Analyst · Abu Dhabi, UAE

        I built this case study to show how I approach service performance: define the service journey, measure it against its own targets, find where demand and delay meet, and turn that into improvement actions.

        1. Data: every 311 request created in the latest full month, the city's published service-level table and a monthly channel history since 2022.
        2. Cleaning: matched each request to its target, removed impossible close times and flagged records with no channel.
        3. Analysis: cycle time, on-time compliance, SLA breaches by volume and delay, backlog ageing and channel shift.
        4. Automation: a scheduled job refreshes the data every week, so the dashboard always shows the latest full month.

        How this was measured

        Sources, matching rules and exclusions

        • Requests: NYC Open Data "311 Service Requests from 2020 to Present" (erm2-nwe9), all requests created in —, refreshed on —.
        • Targets: NYC Open Data "311 Service Level Agreements" (cs9t-e3x8), matched by agency and complaint type; where a type lists several targets, the most common is used. — of requests matched a target.
        • Time to close = closed date minus created date. — requests closed in under one minute, or before they were created, are left out of speed and target figures.
        • Online or app share is measured against requests with a known channel (online, app or direct call to 311). — of requests have no recorded channel.
        • Delay beyond target is the median for requests that missed their target.
